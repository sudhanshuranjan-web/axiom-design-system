Principles

Five rules decide every token and component. When a choice is unclear, they settle it.

Calm by defaultQuiet surfaces, no shadows at rest. Colour appears only where it means something.
One clear next stepEvery view has a single primary action. Nobody wonders what to press.
Colour carries meaningTones label categories, statuses and people. One hue, one meaning per product.
Readable firstText passes 4.5:1. Focus is a thicker line, not colour alone. Motion respects settings.
People and machinesEvery token has a role name and a description, so designers and AI pick the same one.

Start

Building new components

When a screen needs something AXIOM doesn't have yet, this is how to make it so it looks like it was always there. Some things never change, some things are yours to decide, and the order you decide them in matters.

ConstantNever change these, in any component
  • Colour roles. Fill, text, line and action come from --axiom-* roles. No hex values, no new colours. Roles sit on one ramp per hue, 50 to 700: rest 500, hover 600, pressed 700; dark mode steps down to 400 and 300. A fill that carries white text rests one step later when 500 falls under 4.5:1, as the primary does: rest 600, hover and pressed 700.
  • Size scale. Controls are 28 / 32 / 40 on web and 32 / 40 on mobile, where every CTA is 40. Fields 32 web, 44 mobile. Pick a step; never size by padding.
  • 4px grid. Every padding, gap and offset is on the spacing scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40.
  • Even numbers. Every dimension is a multiple of 2: heights, widths, padding, gaps, radii, icon and type sizes. Only strokes are exempt (1px hairlines, 1.5px focus, 1.75 icon stroke). The Dimensions view marks an odd value in orange.
  • Radius roles. 4px on anything you click, 6px on anything that holds content, 8px on panels and dialogs. Inner radius = outer − inset.
  • Type styles. Use the fifteen text styles. Weights 400 and 500 only. Inputs are 16px on mobile.
  • Focus. A 1.5px line in line/focus, never colour alone. Every control is reachable by keyboard.
  • States. Default, hover, pressed, focus, disabled, and where it applies selected, error, loading. Same tokens every time.
  • Motion. Durations and curves come from the motion tokens, and the decision framework decides whether it moves at all.
  • One primary action per view, and a 44 × 44 minimum target on touch.
Yours to decideChange these to fit the job
  • Composition. Which existing parts it combines, and how they are laid out.
  • Density. Which step of the size scale it sits on, matched to its neighbours.
  • Optional parts. Icon, badge, description, trailing action: on or off per use.
  • Width and count. How wide it runs and how many items it shows before it scrolls, wraps or collapses.
  • Tone. Which of the ten tones labels a category, as long as one hue keeps one meaning.
  • Content. Copy, data and illustration. Illustration follows the hairline rules.
  • Mobile variant. Tokens switch on their own; the layout may change (a table becomes rows, a menu becomes a sheet).

Start

The logic, in order

Decide top to bottom. Each step narrows the next, so two people building the same thing end up with the same component.

Nameax-<component>, parts __part, variants --variant. Name it by its job, not its look.
NeverDetach, restyle or copy an existing component to get a new one. Compose it or extend its tokens.
  1. 1
    Search first. Can existing components, composed, do the job? A list row with a trailing switch is not a new component.
  2. 2
    Name the job. One sentence: what people do with it. If it needs two sentences, it is two components.
  3. 3
    Pick the size step. Match what sits beside it: 32 in a web toolbar, 40 in a mobile form. The height sets padding-x and icon size.
  4. 4
    Map colour to roles. Surface for fills, text for words and icons, line for outlines, action for the one thing to press.
  5. 5
    Shape by role. Web: clickable 4, container 6, panel 8. Mobile: CTA 16, chips pill, field 12, card 16–20, sheet 28. Nested corners follow inner = outer − inset.
  6. 6
    Space on the grid. Inline 8 between neighbours, stack 12 between groups, inset 16 inside cards (24 in dialogs).
  7. 7
    List the states. Draw every state before shipping, including empty, loading and error.
  8. 8
    Decide the motion. How often is it seen? Keyboard and high-frequency actions don't animate; the rest use the motion tokens.
  9. 9
    Check mobile. Put it in an .ax-screen: targets ≥ 44, inputs 16px, sheets instead of popovers.
  10. 10
    Document it. When to use, what instead, never-do, and its dimensions (use the Dimensions button on any block).

Each step goes up in hundreds, so hover is always one step past rest and pressed one past hover, stopping at 700. A fill that carries white text rests on 600 when 500 falls under 4.5:1 (the primary: white on indigo-500 is 4.47:1, on 600 it is 6.3:1); hover and pressed then share 700. Dark mode moves two steps down the same ramp (500 → 400 or 300) instead of picking new colours. Neutral alone adds 800, 900 and 950, used only for dark surfaces. Primitives are the source of truth for tokens; screens still use roles.

Foundations

Illustrations

AXIOM illustrations are hairline figures: isometric line drawings built from rounded solids in one stroke, and they answer the pointer. They follow the ten hairline rules, so every new one looks like part of the same set. Move the pointer over each, or press play to watch its tour.

RulesOne object, one idea. Rest is a composition. One bright stroke is the only highlight. No words inside the drawing; names go to the read-out.
NeverFills, gradients, glows, sad faces, logos stamped on boxes, or a timer of its own. Motion is springs (where) and 700ms tweens (which).
Illustration logicHow AXIOM applies it
Find the objectSomething you could put on a desk that does the screen's job: a card box for an inbox, a sieve for search, a pegboard for a sprint. Never a diagram, never an icon.
Draw the absenceEmpty states show the tool holding nothing, composed and whole. The read-out counts to zero: tab 3 · 0.
One answer to the pointerA field (springs, falloff by distance), one of many (700ms tweens, stagger by distance), or a tilt. Hit areas test the rest pose, so nothing flickers.
PalettePlates take surface/raised; strokes are text mixed into the surface at four weights; the one bright stroke is text/link (indigo). Dark mode follows the tokens.
Size and place400 × 320 viewBox, readable at 240px wide. Empty states sit at 240–320px above a heading and one primary button.
Motion costOne shared frame loop that sleeps when still and offscreen, and lands at once when motion is reduced.

Recipes

Press feedback

Everything pressable scales to 0.97 on press in 120 ms, so the interface feels like it heard you.

Origin-aware popover

Popovers grow from their trigger, not from the centre. Modals stay centred.

Asymmetric enter / exit

Enter on a strong ease-out over 220 ms; leave on the exit curve in 160 ms.

Stagger

One entrance per group; 30–50 ms apart and shrinking, never blocking input.

Blur crossfade

A 2 px blur bridges two states so the eye reads one object changing, not two swapping.

Hold to confirm

Slow and linear while you decide (1.6 s), snappy 200 ms ease-out when you let go.

Spring on release

Drag the dot and let go. A spring keeps your throw's velocity and settles without a fixed duration.

Interruptible

Click fast. The transition retargets from where it is; the keyframe restarts from zero.

Clip reveal

clip-path: inset() reveals without layout cost. Used for charts and images entering view.

Grows from its triggertransform-origin follows the button.
Grows from the centreFeels detached from what you pressed.

Tooltip & Popover

Tooltip: a short read-only hint on hover (after 500 ms) or focus (at once), growing from its trigger. Popover: a small interactive panel next to its trigger — focus moves in on open and returns on close.

Overlays

Window, Drawer & Bottom sheet

Window rises 16 px over the scrim and stays centred. Drawer slides in on an iOS-like curve. Bottom sheet can be flicked away — a quick throw dismisses it regardless of distance. Focus is trapped inside and returns on close; Esc closes.

TypesDefault (form) · Alert (irreversible) · Side menu (sectioned settings). Drawer for details beside a list; phones → Bottom sheet.
NeverA window over a window (except an Alert), a Close icon in an Alert, or “OK”/“Yes” as labels.

Overlays

Command palette

Opens with ⌘K (Ctrl K) and finds pages, objects and actions by typing. Results are grouped; arrows move, Enter runs, Esc closes. It opens hundreds of times a day, so it never animates.

NeverA palette without keyboard navigation, or ungrouped results beyond eight.

Mobile

Mobile

Bottom sheet, action sheet & alert

Sheets rise from the bottom with a drag handle and rest at detents: medium (about half) and large. Drag between them or flick; dragging past the top resists; a downward flick dismisses. At the large detent the screen behind shrinks back. Action sheets list choices for one object; alerts confirm the irreversible.

UseSheet for a task tied to this screen (pick, edit, filter). Action sheet for 2–6 actions. Alert only to confirm what can't be undone.
NeverA sheet without a handle, a centred modal for a list of choices, or Delete as the first action.
